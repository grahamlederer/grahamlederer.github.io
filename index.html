About

I have spent more than a decade leading research programs on critical mineral resources and supply chains, and over twenty years doing field geology. My work combines economic geology, geospatial data, and machine learning to help policymakers, federal agencies, industry, and international partners understand where mineral resources are and what it takes to bring them to market.

At the USGS I led the DARPA-funded Critical Mineral Assessments with AI Support (CriticalMAAS) program, which built AI/ML tools for geologic maps, mineral reports, prospectivity models, and human–machine interfaces. I also led the MINFRAME project on methods for quantitative mineral resource assessment and critical-minerals workforce training, advised the Earth Mapping Resources Initiative (Earth MRI), and was a core contributor to the 2017 U.S. Critical Minerals List. Today I build software for the geosciences at Jataware.

My research started in tectonics: monazite petrochronology of Himalayan granites, Blue Ridge basement structure, rare earth mineralization in the Mojave, and high-precision U–Pb geochronology of the Siberian Traps.
